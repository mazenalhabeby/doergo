Proposal · the big-app model

How WhatsApp, Telegram and Slack keep requests near zero

1 · One live connection for everything

Send, receive, typing, read ticks and online status all travel on the connection we already have (Socket.IO). Every send gets an "arrived" reply, so nothing is lost.

2 · A copy on the device

Chats and messages are kept on the phone (its offline database) and in the browser's storage. Opening the app shows everything at once, with no request.

3 · Catch-up by number

Every change for a person gets a rising number. Back from a tunnel or sleep, the app asks once: "everything after 1532". One new table on the server.

4 · Photos straight to storage, push only when closed

Media never passes through the chat server. No push while the app is connected.

We take

The live connection for everything, the device copy, catch-up by number, photos straight to storage.

We don't take

WhatsApp's own server language and binary protocol (built for billions; ours handles thousands). End-to-end encryption (the company must be able to see its work chats, as in Slack and Teams). Deleting messages after delivery (the company needs the history).
