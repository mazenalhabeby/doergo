Where the complexity comes from

Five things that make it hard

“Away” means six things

Remote bucketaway dayREMOTE requestallowRemotepresence REMOTEapproval “away”

The last one is really “left the site area during the shift”. Workers and leaders meet the same word for different rules.

Four answers to “is there a shift?”

workModelrotaweekly schedulescheduleType

Plus the no-shift policy and the automatic clock’s mode on top. A rota at a workspace set to “no model” is ignored by the clock but may still chase a no-show.

Twice the same idea

rest rulesauto restextra timeovertime

Two rest systems. Two routes for the same overtime round, behind two different options. Rejecting one only nudges; rejecting the other clocks the worker out.

Asking what it already knows

left at 16:42shift herelunch window

“When did you leave?” — the phone saw it. “Which workspace?” — the rota says. “Why did you leave?” — it is lunch time.

Dead-end refusals

OUTSIDE_GEOFENCEAWAY_NOT_ALLOWEDGPS accuracy too low

Standing at a customer’s site, the worker is told no — with nothing they can do next except phone the office.

The idea

The phone knows where you are. The server knows your shift.

So the worker is asked only what nobody else knows. Every office rule stays: geofence, rota, rests, approvals, two clocks.

Ask only what we can’t know

Place, shift, the moment you left: inferred. A reason for something unusual: asked, once.

One “away”

On site, on a planned rest, or away during work — three states a worker can read. The old words stay in the database, not on the screen.

One request for more time

Extra time and overtime become one request. A “no” means the time after the shift is not counted — never a surprise clock-out.

A daily prompt budget

At most four pushes about the clock in a normal day. A reminder repeats only when the worker hasn’t seen the first one.

Your decisions

Four questions before anything is built

1 · Lunch off site counts as the rest?

Leaving inside the planned rest window starts the rest automatically; no “you left the work area”.

2 · One request for more time, never a forced clock-out?

Merge extra time and overtime; a “no” stops counting after the shift end instead of ending the shift.

3 · A daily budget of four clock pushes?

Repeat a reminder only when the first wasn’t opened; tell the leader once.

4 · Fix the five bugs first?

They need no design decision and make today’s flow honest while the rest is built.
