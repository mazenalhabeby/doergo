⑧The tile
ONE TILE, SIX LINES
the ringHow far through the shift. clockInAt → expectedClockOutAt.
the dotWhat state. Solid jade on, hollow warn break, crit late.
the nameFirst name only. A row of initials is a puzzle.
the placeWhich workspace — the thing that decides whether you can walk over.
the figureTime left, not time worked. Every other attendance surface shows hours worked; a manager at 13:20 needs to know Pia is free in 25 minutes.
the spanClock-in → expected out, 9.5pt mono. Evidence under the claim.
⚠️ No arc when expectedClockOutAt is null, or when endIsDailyLimit — that is a cut-off, not a finish, and an arc at 90% would read “nearly done”. Plain jade ring: present, no progress.
⑨The two rules
RULE 1 · THE RAIL IS NOT A ROSTER
A tile exists only when there is something to decide about that person.
Late — somebody has to ring them.
Free soon — finishing inside the hour; the next job can go to them.
Over — past the shift end, overtime accruing now.
Break over — past its allowance.
Everyone steady is a chip with a count. So the card’s height tracks how much is happening, not how many people there are. Two hundred people on a calm morning is a short card; nine people with three problems is a long one.
RULE 2 · THE UNIT IS DERIVED, NOT CHOSEN
1 workspacePeople. You know all their names.
2–3People, with the site on each tile.
4+Workspaces. 62 of 148 is not a number anybody can act on; “three sites are short” is.
A site row is the same card in miniature — a count, a presence bar, what is wrong. Tap one and the people come back, scoped. One registry row, one component; the unit is a useMemo over scopeSpaceIds.length.
⑩Who sees what
SEEING AND ACTING ARE THREE GRANTS
canViewSpaceAttendanceThe whole card, read only. All three endpoints carry it — checked on the gateway controller, not taken from the doc.
+ canReconcileAttendanceExcuse a no-show, inside /manage/attendance.
+ canApproveOvertimeApprove an over-run shift.
One verb on the card: Call, which needs no permission — you are dialling a phone. Everything that changes a record lives behind the exception rows, gated separately.
⚠️ canViewSpaceAttendance does not imply the roster. Treat location assignments as optional data — which is finding 02.
⑪What is still wrong in the code
MEASURED IN THE REPO
01getActiveEntries is unbounded.prisma.timeEntry.findMany with an orderBy and no take, on a route documented “org-wide”. At twelve sites that is ~148 rows on every home render to draw one number. listNoShows in the same file uses take: 200, and §3.7 asks page=1&limit=1 — the pattern exists twice and this route never got it.
02The denominator collapses into the numerator.if (rostered.size === 0) for (const id of clockedInUserIds) rostered.add(id). “6 of 9” becomes “6 of 6” and nobody is ever missing. Truthful as a list, not as a ratio — and §3.6 asks for a ratio. Drop the “/n” when there is no roster in scope.
03Two cases where a progress arc would lie.expectedClockOutAt is null on task/none spaces. endIsDailyLimit means the workspace’s daily cap for clocking in without a shift, not a shift end. Plain jade ring in both.
04listNoShows(days = 7).§3.8 asks about this morning. Worse: a week never empties, so §3.8’s own “hidden when empty” stops working and the band is open every day. Pass 1.
01 and 02 ship alone, before any widget work — they fix the admin home that is live today.
Cost is low: getActiveEntries, getActiveBreaks, countPendingApprovals, listNoShows and resolveNoShow all already exist, and admin-dashboard.tsx (1,089 lines) already renders a version of this. Extraction, not construction.
